Generate NPPE-style multiple-choice exam questions for Canadian engineering licensure preparation.

  "questions": [
    {

      "options": ["...", "...", "...", "..."],

    }

---

TOTAL QUESTIONS

Generate exactly X questions total, where X is provided externally.

If X is not explicitly provided, assume X = 110.

---

PROPORTIONAL DISTRIBUTION RULE

Maintain the following category weight ratios:

- PROFESSIONALISM: 10
- ETHICS: 20
- PROFESSIONAL_PRACTICE: 30
- LAW_FOR_PROFESSIONAL_PRACTICE: 25
- PROFESSIONAL_LAW: 10
- DISCIPLINE_AND_REGULATION: 15

Total weight = 110

You MUST scale these proportions to match 50.

---

ALLOCATION METHOD (MANDATORY)

1. Compute:
   category_count = round((category_weight / 110) * X)

2. Adjust rounding so that:
   - Total sum of all categories = X exactly
   - If there is rounding error:
     - Add/subtract remaining questions starting from highest-weight categories in order:
       PROFESSIONAL_PRACTICE → LAW_FOR_PROFESSIONAL_PRACTICE → ETHICS → DISCIPLINE_AND_REGULATION → PROFESSIONALISM → PROFESSIONAL_LAW

3. Final result MUST match X exactly.

QUESTION STYLE RULES (HARD MODE)

- Questions must be highly challenging and exam-caliber (NPPE professional licensing level)
- Every question must be scenario-based with realistic Canadian engineering contexts
- Scenarios must include layered constraints (legal + ethical + contractual + safety conflicts)
- Require deep judgment, not recall or definitions
- Avoid obvious clue wording
- Avoid telegraphing correct answers
- Include ambiguity where multiple answers are defensible, but only ONE is best under professional standards
- Distractors must be extremely close in correctness:
  - All options must be plausible in real engineering practice
  - All options must be professionally defensible at first glance
  - Differences must be subtle (standard-of-care, hierarchy of duty, jurisdictional nuance)
  - Avoid obviously wrong answers

Include scenarios involving:
- supervisor vs junior engineer conflicts
- client pressure vs public safety obligations
- regulatory reporting dilemmas
- cost/schedule vs safety trade-offs
- uncertainty and incomplete information
- document control and professional sign-off responsibilities
- inter-professional conflicts
- whistleblowing thresholds
